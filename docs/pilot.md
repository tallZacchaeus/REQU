# Running the pilot

One department, real requisitions, small amounts, about a month — with whatever you do today
still running alongside. The point is not to prove the software works; it is to find out what
the workflow gets wrong while changing it is still cheap.

## Before anyone is invited

**1. Decide who plays each part.** Four people minimum, and they must be different people:

| Part | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| Head of Department | Raises requisitions and files the receipts afterwards |
| Assistant National Youth Pastor | Recommends, or sends it back with changes |
| National Youth Pastor | Approves, returns or rejects |
| Finance | Releases the money and accepts the receipts |

Nobody can do two of these on the same requisition — the system refuses it, deliberately.
A Head of Department cannot approve their own request, and nobody signs off their own
reconciliation.

**2. Ask each of them to sign in once**, at https://requisition.rccgyayang.org, using their
**@rccgyayang.org** address. No other address works. They will get a link by email, and land
on a page saying they are registered but have no part yet. That is correct.

**3. Give them their part.** Sign in as the administrator, open **People**, and set each
person's role — and the department for the Head of Department. They can reload and carry on.

## During the month

- **Start with something small and real.** A genuine requisition for a modest amount beats an
  invented one for a large amount: people behave differently when it is real.
- **Do not switch the old process off.** Run both. If the app loses something, nothing is lost.
- **Watch the returns.** A requisition sent back for changes usually means the form asked for
  the wrong thing, or asked for it badly. That is the most valuable signal you will get.
- **Ask the quiet ones.** The person who has not used it is telling you something.

## What to write down

Keep a note of anything that made somebody hesitate, ask a question, or do it wrong. Those are
worth more than a list of features. Specifically:

- Anywhere somebody had to ask what a word meant.
- Anything they expected to be able to do and could not.
- Anything they did by accident.
- How long each stage actually took, against how long it feels like it should.

## At the end

Decide three things:

1. **Is the workflow right?** Five stages, four roles, receipts to close. If it is wrong, it is
   far cheaper to change now than after eighty people have learned it.
2. **What must change before anyone else uses it?**
3. **Who else goes next** — one more department, not all of them.

## Things to know while piloting

- **The site has a holding password** while it is not yet open to everybody. Pilot users need it
  once, in the browser prompt, before the app's own sign-in. It comes off when you are ready to
  open up.
- **Receipts must be attached before a requisition can be closed.** PDF or a photo, up to 10 MB.
- **Nothing is deleted.** A requisition that should not have been raised is rejected, and the
  rejection stays on the record. That is the point of it.
- **Everyone gets an email when something is waiting on them.** If people say they were not
  told, check that first — it usually means an address is wrong on their record.

## If something goes wrong

The person to tell is whoever holds `webmaster@rccgyayang.org`. Useful facts to include: who
was signed in, what they were trying to do, what the screen said, and the requisition reference
(`REQ-2026-001`). The activity trail on the requisition records every step and cannot be
edited, so the history is always there to look at.
